Research question

What does a compliant spine contribute?

Biological quadrupeds bend through their trunks, while most small robotic quadrupeds use rigid bodies. The study isolates spinal compliance as the main variable by comparing a rigid morphology against a passive roll-spine version under aligned training conditions.

Methodology

Controlled experiments in learned locomotion

01 /
Standardize the task

Use the same velocity-tracking objective and comparable robot/actuator settings.

02 /
Train with PPO

Train learned locomotion in parallelized MuJoCo/rsl_rl environments using reward terms for forward motion, uprightness and smoothness.

03 /
Hold confounds constant

Keep policy structure, action space, actuator configuration and evaluation procedure aligned across morphologies.

04 /
Analyze impacts

Compare ground-reaction/landing force behavior and velocity tracking during simulated locomotion.

Result

Softer contact without losing forward motion

The passive roll-spine configuration reduced mean landing force by about 11% while maintaining forward walking performance in the evaluated simulation conditions.
