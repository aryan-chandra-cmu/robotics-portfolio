Research focus

One motion, multiple robot bodies

Imitation learning requires physically meaningful reference motions. A canine trajectory cannot be directly replayed on every four-legged robot: link lengths, actuator definitions, kinematic loops, joint frames, and permissible contacts differ.

Pipeline

Reference motion to validation-ready data

01 /
Motion extraction

Convert canine motion sequences into trackable body, foot, and gait-phase references.

02 /
Morphology calibration

Establish links, coordinate frames, joint mapping, and relevant closed-chain constraints for each robot.

03 /
Retargeting + IK

Solve for robot-specific joint trajectories while respecting each morphology’s geometry and range.

04 /
Contact-aware grounding

Refine foot heights and contact timing to avoid foot penetration and infeasible poses.

05 /
Validation + replay

Run numerical Jacobian checks, constraint verification, contact diagnostics, and reference visualization in MuJoCo/MJLab.

Technical difficulty

Most errors are in the conventions

  • Coordinate frames: different local frames can flip or rotate a seemingly correct foot target.
  • Closed-chain linkage: individual joint angles must satisfy coupling constraints rather than independent open-chain IK.
  • Contact timing: trajectories need physically plausible stance and swing intervals.
  • Repeatable evaluation: variant × skill tests should catch regressions across robot morphologies.

This page documents the reference-generation and validation pipeline. It does not claim a fully deployed imitation policy on physical hardware.
