01 / Robot learning problem

Making a real dog's gait feasible for a four-bar robot

Motion capture from a canine is a powerful locomotion prior, but an anatomical dog's three-dimensional paw path is not automatically executable on a compact Microtaur. In particular, the Microtaur four-bar leg does not provide shoulder/hip abduction-adduction. Copying the dog's lateral paw trajectory produces unreachable commands, broken contact timing, or incorrectly mapped limbs.

I developed a robot-aware pipeline to convert canine pace motion into physically interpretable Microtaur reference trajectories, including source-paw extraction, phase mapping, calibrated inverse kinematics, linkage-aware correction, root/foot trajectory packaging, and MJLab tracking-task integration. This archive contains concrete rigid- and active-spine workflows from a broader effort exploring four quadruped morphologies.

02 / Motion-to-policy data architecture

From canine keypoints to a robot-specific tracking reference

3D canine paw & body motion→Leg identity + phase mapping→Planar X–Z foot targets→Constrained four-bar IK→Contact + continuity checks→MJLab NPZ motion
01 /
Inspect and extract dog motion

Load raw canine data and inspect the paw/torso kinematics. Retain the original 3D source motion for analysis rather than discarding the lateral component prematurely.

02 /
Calibrate robot coordinate frames

Resolve the robot's anatomical front (+X toward the battery), left/right orientation, joint naming and leg order. For the rigid Microtaur the source [LF, RF, LR, RR] legs map to robot [BR, BL, FL, FR] through [3, 2, 0, 1].

03 /
Project onto the reachable workspace

Track fore/aft X and vertical Z, but hold each foot's Y at its own nominal left/right stance offset. The pipeline explicitly avoids commanding unattainable lateral canine motion, and avoids collapsing all feet onto the Y = 0 centerline.

04 /
Refine linkage-aware joint trajectories

Produce joint positions from four-bar kinematics, refine planar foot error, and check actuator mapping, foot clearance, stride direction and equality constraints. The rigid-body tools also reconstruct cyclic velocities and remove repeated endpoints.

05 /
Export and inspect in simulation

Build time-indexed NPZ references containing robot-root poses and joint states. Use kinematic and PD playback tools to inspect tracking, start-phase continuity, contact slip, saturation, and the gap between nominal reference motion and simulated execution.

03 / Modeling decisions

The debugging details that made the transfer interpretable

Correct sagittal projection

Each leg retains its own nominal Y; only X and Z enter the planar tracking objective. This respects the leg's missing ab/adduction DOF.

Coordinate-frame and phase checks

For rigid Microtaur, a battery-forward stance cycle must touch down toward +X and sweep toward −X during ground contact.

Closed-loop motion playback

The rigid PD player includes cyclic-velocity reconstruction, smooth ramp-in, capped feedforward, passive damping and slip/saturation diagnostics.

Separate morphology tools

Rigid and active-spine retargeters and IK refiners preserve morphology-specific conventions instead of reusing an incompatible universal mapping.

The archive's README_MAPPING.md documents the precise leg order and front/back conventions; conversion and validation scripts form a reproducible analysis path. The same archive includes generated .npz motion artifacts for rigid and active-spine variants.

Engineering lesson: In robot learning, a reference motion that visually resembles the source can still be wrong if it violates the target robot's joint topology, contact kinematics or frame conventions. Here those assumptions are documented and testable.

04 / MJLab imitation learning integration

Implemented the tracking task—not just a motion converter

The research folder includes a registered MJLab task (Mjlab-Tracking-Flat-Microtaur), a reference-motion command configuration, dedicated robot-specific reward functions, and an rsl_rl PPO runner configuration. The initial curriculum intentionally uses nominal dynamics, with push/disturbance and other randomization disabled while first-stage reference tracking is established.

Custom exponential rewards score eight driven joint positions, joint velocities and planar X–Z foot positions. The configuration also penalizes rapid action changes and joint-limit violations. Small reset perturbations and tight foot/root deviation terminations are used for the first-stage tracking task.

Status, stated precisely: the archive verifies the software architecture, source-to-robot data pipeline, motion artifacts, and imitation-task configuration. It does not include a completed learning curve or held-out result proving that PPO converged to a robust canine-imitating policy. Those results remain a next experimental milestone.

05 / Research impact & evidence

Why this work matters for humanoid and legged robot learning

  • Retargeting across embodiment differences: translating demonstrations across mismatched anatomy, DOFs, linkages and coordinate frames is the core barrier for reusable robot motion data.
  • ML infrastructure meets robotics: the deliverable is not just an animation—it is reference data, validation code, and a registered training environment with explicit rewards and termination criteria.
  • Experimental discipline: isolating reference-generation correctness before policy training prevents expensive RL runs from learning around broken geometry or mislabeled actions.
  • Next milestones: quantify kinematic tracking errors; train/evaluate the configured imitation policy; add disturbance/domain randomization; and investigate physical-hardware feasibility.
